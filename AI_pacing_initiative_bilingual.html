AI 減速倡議：誰受益、市場如何理解、未來如何發展

The AI Pacing Initiative: Who Benefits, How Markets Read It and What Comes Next

從 Simunovic 等人的先發制人實驗，理解為什麼各國明知 AI 競賽有風險，仍然難以單方面減速。

Using Simunovic et al.'s preemptive-strike experiment to explain why countries struggle to slow AI competition unilaterally despite recognising its risks.

核心判斷

Dario Amodei 提出的不是「停止 AI」，而是讓能力增長受到審核、門檻和國家協調約束。他明確表示進步仍會很快，且美國不能因減速而失去對中國的領先。1

因此，這項倡議同時具有三種性質：它是一項真實的安全主張，也是一種由政府協助解決產業囚徒困境的要求，還是一套維持美國及現有前沿企業優勢的競爭框架。

最可能的結果不是全球 AI 投資停止，而是市場結構改變：前沿模型的發布與訓練受到更多審核，但晶片、算力、能源、人才及國家級 AI 基礎設施的競爭繼續。

一、Simunovic 等人的先發制人實驗

實驗設計

Simunovic、Mifune 和 Yamagishi 研究的是一種「先發制人博弈」：兩名參與者各自面前都有一個按鈕，可以選擇是否先攻擊對方。若雙方都不按鈕，兩人都得到最高報酬；若其中一人先按，攻擊者付出100日圓成本，被攻擊者損失1,000日圓，而且被攻擊者不能反擊。先按按鈕不會創造新的財富，反而會降低雙方總收益。3

因此，雙方共同克制是整體上最好的結果。但對個別參與者而言，如果相信對方可能先按，等待就可能承受更大損失。這使「先按」成為一種防禦性選擇，而不一定代表參與者喜歡攻擊。

實驗結果

當兩名參與者都具備攻擊能力時，32人中有16人選擇攻擊，比例為50%。所有選擇攻擊的人都在第一秒內按下按鈕，顯示行為主要是對潛在威脅的快速反應，而不是長時間計算後的複雜策略。

研究者另設置了對照條件：當對手根本沒有攻擊能力時，26人中只有1人選擇攻擊。3 這個差異說明，攻擊並非單純由獲利動機造成；真正改變行為的是對手同時具有能力，而參與者又無法確定對手是否會先行動。

實驗結果與AI競爭的對照

在國家級AI競爭中，實驗裡的「攻擊按鈕」可以對應到前沿模型、算力、晶片、網絡攻防和軍民兩用AI能力。對手是否按鈕，則對應一國是否秘密訓練更強模型、突破模型能力門檻，或先把AI部署到軍事和情報系統。

實驗元素 國家級AI競爭中的對應
對手具有攻擊能力 對手擁有先進模型、算力和軍民兩用AI能力
無法知道對手何時行動 難以核實對手是否秘密訓練或部署更強模型
等待可能失去先機 擔心錯過技術突破、軍事窗口或產業標準
先行動可避免被動承受損失 先取得模型、晶片、人才和算力優勢
雙方都行動會降低總收益 AI軍備競賽提高成本、事故和失控風險
雙方都不行動是整體較佳結果 共同減速、共享安全標準和互相核查

這解釋了為什麼AI發展會出現「明知有風險，仍然繼續投入」的僵持狀態。美國不必認為中國一定會立刻攻擊自己，才會擴大算力；中國也不必認為美國一定會用AI攻擊自己，才會發展自主晶片和本土模型。只要雙方都相信對手具有取得重大優勢的能力，又無法排除對方秘密加速的可能，就會把「自己減速、對手加速」視為最危險的結果。

共同減速可能是整體最佳結果，但只要缺乏可信核查，單方面減速就可能被視為把戰略優勢交給對手。

二、誰是既得利益者，倡議背後有什麼動機

1. 現有前沿 AI 公司是最直接的潛在受益者

Anthropic、OpenAI、Google DeepMind和xAI已經擁有模型、人才、資料、算力供應及融資能力。若政府要求第三方評估、事故通報、能力測試及持續監管，合規成本會大幅增加。大型企業能夠承擔這些固定成本，初創公司和開源團隊則較困難。

因此，安全規則可能同時產生兩種效果。第一，它降低災難性事故風險。第二，它把現有領先者的技術優勢轉化為監管護城河。

Amodei 建議讓外部評估者取得接近員工的長期存取權，並要求其他前沿公司採用相同安排。1 這有助於建立可信安全標準，但標準若主要由領先公司協助設計，也可能把它們現有的組織和技術模式變成市場准入條件。

這不證明倡議者的安全憂慮不真誠。較準確的判斷是：真實安全動機與商業利益可以同時存在。

2. Anthropic 希望消除「率先減速者受罰」的問題

若Anthropic單方面放慢模型訓練，競爭者可能搶走用戶、人才、融資和技術領先。Simunovic等人的先發制人實驗說明，即使各方都不想造成損失，只要對手擁有先行動能力，恐懼便足以引發防禦性行動。3

因此，Amodei要求的並非單純自我約束，而是讓政府要求所有主要企業遵守相同規則。這等於把企業自身無法解決的囚徒困境交給政府：

如果所有競爭者都被迫減速，安全公司就不必以失去市場作為謹慎的代價。

這也是倡議的策略價值。它把「Anthropic應否減速」改寫為「整個產業應否在共同規則下減速」。

3. 大型雲端平台可能從資本開支下降中受益

Microsoft、Alphabet、Meta和Amazon已投入龐大資本建設資料中心。如果能力增長放慢，它們可以延後部分新增建設，繼續利用既有設備提供推理、雲端和應用服務。收入不一定同步下降，但折舊以外的新資本支出和現金消耗可能減少。

這種情況有利於已擁有大型算力資產、客戶渠道和軟體生態的平台。相反，仍依賴外部融資建設產能的雲端新進者較脆弱。

不過，利益並不完全一致。若減速同時降低雲端 AI 使用量，平台收入也會受壓。因此，大型平台最理想的情境不是AI停滯，而是訓練競賽降溫、既有模型商業化加速。

4. 美國政府和盟友希望把安全治理與維持領先結合

Amodei一方面提出全球協調，另一方面主張限制高性能晶片和半導體設備流向中國，打擊晶片走私、海外資料中心遠端存取、模型蒸餾和模型權重竊取。1

從美國角度看，這套安排可在保留技術領先的前提下，為國內公司建立安全緩衝期。從中國角度看，它卻很容易被理解為：美國希望先限制中國追趕，再利用現有優勢制定全球規則。

中國外交部把有關主張批評為製造恐懼和惡性競爭；中國官媒則將其描述為遏制中國的AI冷戰框架。6 這種反應會降低全球協議的可能性，並促使中國加快國產晶片、模型和算力體系建設。

5. 評估、安全和合規產業會形成新市場

若常駐評估者、模型審計、紅隊測試、算力申報、訓練環境監測和事故通報成為強制要求，第三方評估機構、AI安全公司、網絡安全公司和合規軟體供應商會直接受益。

這類需求不依賴模型能力是否持續高速增長。競賽越激烈，政府越需要監測；管制越嚴，企業越需要合規。因此，它們是「加速」和「受管制減速」兩種情境都可能受益的次級賽道。

6. 主要承壓者

最直接承壓的是依賴持續擴大資本開支的供應商，包括AI加速器、客製化晶片、數據中心網絡設備、AI雲端新進者和部分數據中心建設鏈。如果訓練週期延長或大型客戶消化現有產能，這些公司的收入預期會比平台公司的應用收入更快下修。

較小的模型公司和開源開發者也可能承受不成比例的合規成本。若規則根據算力、模型能力或資安風險設置許可門檻，市場可能進一步集中於少數大型實驗室。

三、市場如何反應和理解

1. 市場首先出售「AI資本開支永遠高速增長」的假設

倡議在2026年9月12日週末公布後，9月14日首個交易日出現明顯分化。以下是Yahoo Finance日線收市價計算的單日報酬：7

半導體和AI雲端基建公司的跌幅顯著大於大盤，而Alphabet、Microsoft和Meta上升。市場因此不是簡單地判斷「AI沒有價值」，而是在重新分配價值：從出售新增算力的人，轉向已經擁有算力並可降低未來支出的平台。

《Fortune》引述的市場解讀亦指出，如果AI進步速度下降，Nvidia和CoreWeave等基礎設施供應商更容易受損；大型平台則可以停止部分新增建設，消化已有產能並提高自由現金流。4

2. 市場沒有把倡議理解為可信的全面停工

Amodei、Altman和Musk雖然支持放慢，但沒有宣布停止模型訓練。Altman明確表示，「pacing」不是停止，技術進步仍會繼續。5

所以市場初步定價的是三種風險，而不是永久停止AI：

  1. 前沿訓練週期可能拉長；
  2. 監管和安全開支可能上升；
  3. 長期算力需求的斜率可能降低。

這也解釋為何平台公司沒有全面下跌。投資者認為AI商業化仍會繼續，只是未來價值不一定持續集中在新增GPU和資料中心建設。

3. 這次價格變動不能全部歸因於減速倡議

9月14日市場同時受到油價上升、美國十年期國債收益率接近5%及加息預期影響。4 S&P 500和Nasdaq當日均下跌，因此不能把所有科技股變動都視為倡議的因果結果。

不過，半導體指數下跌5.86%，明顯超過S&P 500的0.48%跌幅；三家大型平台逆市上升。這種橫向分化與「AI資本開支下降、平台現金流改善」的解釋一致。它是有力的事件訊號，但不是乾淨的因果實驗。

四、未來會如何發展

最可能路徑：有選擇地減速，總體能力競賽繼續

近期不太可能出現全球統一的訓練暫停。美國企業之間缺乏完全信任，美國和中國之間更缺乏可驗證承諾。CFR的分析同樣把企業競爭和美中競爭描述為兩層相互嵌套的囚徒困境。8

因此，政策最可能集中於可執行的程序性要求：常駐第三方評估、能力門檻測試、重大事故通報、訓練算力申報、模型權重保護及高風險部署限制。這些措施會延長模型推出時間，但不必然減少研發預算。

美國會把「安全監管」與「對華競爭」綁在一起

美國國內可能出現兩股力量。一方要求對所有前沿實驗室建立一致的聯邦規則，防止企業因自願減速而失去競爭力。另一方擔心監管削弱美國相對中國的領先，因此反對硬性速度限制。美國總統已公開表示減速可能危及對華優勢。5

最後較可能形成折衷：對國內模型增加審核，但同時擴大算力、能源和半導體供應，並加強對中國的技術限制。 這會降低部分部署風險，卻未必降低國家級能力競賽。

中國會加強自主，而不是接受由美國領先者設計的減速框架

中國本身也關注AI失控、網絡安全和政權安全。中國領導層曾強調AI應保持在人類控制之下；中國國家安全部門亦警告外國模型可能威脅政治和意識形態安全。6

但安全關切不會自動轉化為減速。當倡議同時包含限制中國取得晶片和模型的內容，中國更可能把自主研發視為防禦措施。結果可能是兩套技術體系加速形成，各自在內部加強監管，對外則維持競爭。

產業價值會從「訓練速度」部分轉向「可部署能力」

若前沿訓練受到限制，企業會更重視提高現有模型的推理效率、可靠性和商業使用率。資金可能由純粹追求最大訓練集群，部分轉向：

  • 推理成本下降和模型壓縮；
  • 企業應用、代理工作流和垂直軟體；
  • 模型安全、監測、身份及權限控制；
  • 數據中心利用率、電力效率及網絡優化；
  • 國防、政府和主權AI基礎設施。

所以，「減速」未必減少AI總支出，更可能改變支出結構。

五、三種情境

情境 核心特徵 主要受益者 主要承壓者
選擇性管制，持續競賽 增加審計與發布門檻，但訓練投資繼續 大型平台、前沿實驗室、安全合規、國防AI 小型模型公司、部分開源項目
安全事件推動強制減速 重大事故後，政府限制大型訓練或部署 既有領先者、大型平台、評估機構 晶片、AI雲端新進者、數據中心建設鏈
美中競爭壓倒安全協調 美國拒絕實質減速，中國加速自主 晶片、能源、主權算力、國防科技 全球治理、跨境技術合作，整體系統安全

目前最合理的基準情境是第一種。第二種需要新的重大安全事故或明確能力失控證據。第三種則可能與第一種同時發生：國內監管更嚴，但國際競爭更快。

六、應觀察的訊號

判斷倡議是否真正改變產業，不能只看企業領袖表態。應觀察以下可驗證指標：

  1. Anthropic、OpenAI、Google及xAI是否延後大型訓練或模型發布，而不只是增加安全測試；
  2. 大型雲端平台是否下調資本開支指引或延後資料中心項目；
  3. 美國是否建立全國一致的前沿模型審核與算力申報框架；
  4. 美國是否進一步限制晶片出口、海外算力租用及模型權重流動；
  5. 中國是否提出可接受外部核查的對等安排，或轉向更強的自主替代；
  6. 2026年9月24日預定的美中領導人會面，是否產生具體AI風險溝通或核查機制。8

七、最終判斷

最大的既得利益者是現有前沿AI公司、大型雲端平台，以及未來的安全審計和合規產業。 其共同利益是把無限制的速度競賽，轉為只有具備資本、技術和合規能力的大型參與者才能進行的受控競賽。

Amodei的主要公開動機是避免AI能力超越人類的控制與安全準備。1 但其方案也可以保護Anthropic的安全品牌，迫使競爭者共同承擔減速成本，提高新進者門檻，並維持美國對中國的領先。這些動機不是互斥的。

市場已迅速理解這一點。它首先下調算力供應鏈的持續高增長假設，同時提高部分大型平台降低資本開支、消化既有產能的價值。4、7

未來不會是簡單的「加速或停止」。更可能是：

模型發布受到更多限制，產業集中度提高，美中算力競爭加劇，AI總投入繼續增長，但資金由盲目擴大訓練部分轉向部署、安全、效率和國家級基礎設施。

The bottom line

Dario Amodei is not asking the artificial-intelligence industry to stop. He is arguing that the most powerful models should advance behind tougher evaluations, clearer capability thresholds and rules that bind the leading developers at the same time.1, 2

That distinction matters. A voluntary pause by one company would hand an opening to its rivals. A national slowdown could do the same for another country. The result is a contest in which nearly every participant can see the danger, yet few can afford to move first.

The likeliest outcome is therefore not an end to AI investment. It is a reshuffling of where the money goes. Frontier training and model releases would face more scrutiny, while spending on chips, power, data centers, security and sovereign AI infrastructure would continue.

1. A laboratory experiment explains the race

The rules of the game

Simunovic, Mifune and Yamagishi designed a pre-emptive-strike game in which two participants could attack one another by pressing a button. If neither pressed, both kept the highest payoff. If one pressed first, the attacker paid 100 yen, the target lost 1,000 yen and could not retaliate. An attack created no new value. It simply made the combined outcome worse.3

Mutual restraint was plainly the best result. But waiting carried a risk: if the other person struck first, the loss would be much larger than the cost of launching a pre-emptive attack.

Fear, not profit, drove the attacks

When both participants could attack, 16 of 32—exactly half—pressed the button. Every attacker acted within the first second. In a control condition, where the opponent had no ability to strike, only one of 26 participants attacked.3

The gap is the important finding. Participants were not attacking because aggression paid. They attacked because an opponent had the capacity to hurt them and because they could not know the opponent’s intentions.

The AI parallel

For governments, the equivalent of the button is not a single weapon. It is a package of capabilities: frontier models, large computing clusters, advanced chips, cyber tools and military applications. A rival’s intentions are hard to observe, and much of the work can proceed before outsiders see a finished system.

Experimental condition AI competition equivalent
The opponent can attack A rival possesses advanced models, compute and dual-use AI capabilities
The opponent’s intention is unknown Training and deployment can be difficult to verify from outside
Waiting may sacrifice the initiative A country fears missing a technical breakthrough, military window or industry standard
Acting first limits exposure A country races to secure chips, models, talent and compute
Mutual action lowers the combined payoff The race raises costs and increases the risk of accidents or loss of control
Mutual restraint is collectively preferable Common safety rules and reciprocal verification reduce the pressure to race

Neither Washington nor Beijing needs to expect an immediate AI attack for this logic to apply. Each side only needs to believe that the other could gain a decisive economic or security advantage. Under those conditions, “we slow down while they accelerate” becomes the outcome both sides fear most.

A coordinated slowdown may be safer for everyone. Without credible verification, however, unilateral restraint can look like strategic surrender.

2. The initiative would favour incumbents

Frontier labs can afford the rulebook

Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind and xAI already control scarce talent, models, data, computing capacity and financing. Mandatory outside evaluations, incident reports, capability tests and continuous monitoring would raise fixed costs. Large laboratories can absorb those costs more easily than startups or open-source teams.

The same rules can serve two purposes. They can reduce the risk of a severe AI failure, and they can turn the leaders’ existing advantages into a regulatory moat.

Amodei has proposed sustained access for external evaluators and comparable obligations for other frontier companies.1 Such measures could make safety claims more credible. They could also make the operating practices of today’s largest laboratories the price of admission for everyone else.

These conclusions are not contradictory. A company can be sincerely concerned about safety while benefiting commercially from the rules it supports.

Anthropic wants rivals to share the cost of caution

If Anthropic slows alone, competitors can pursue its customers, engineers, investors and technical lead. The pre-emptive-strike experiment captures the problem: fear can produce defensive action even when no participant wants the mutually damaging outcome.3

Amodei’s proposal therefore depends on collective restraint, not corporate virtue. Government would establish rules that apply across the industry.

If every frontier laboratory faces the same constraints, a cautious company no longer pays the full competitive price of slowing down.

The policy question is no longer whether Anthropic should move more slowly. It is whether governments can create a system in which all major laboratories do so together.

Cloud platforms could spend less without abandoning AI

Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta and Amazon have committed enormous sums to data centers. If frontier progress takes longer, they could defer some construction while using existing capacity for inference, cloud services and applications. Revenue need not fall as quickly as capital spending.

That would favour companies that already own computing assets, customer relationships and software ecosystems. New cloud providers that still depend on outside financing would be more vulnerable.

The platforms do not want AI to stall. Their preferred outcome is a slower training race paired with faster commercial use of the capacity and models they already have.

Washington is tying safety to competition with China

Amodei supports international coordination, but he also backs restrictions on China’s access to advanced chips and semiconductor equipment. He has called for action against chip smuggling, remote access to overseas data centers, model distillation and the theft of model weights.1

For Washington, that combination offers a way to impose stronger domestic safeguards without surrendering the U.S. lead. For Beijing, it looks like an attempt to restrict China first and write the rules second.

China’s Foreign Ministry has criticised the argument as fearmongering and “vicious competition,” while Chinese state media have cast it as part of an AI Cold War.6 That reaction makes a global pact less likely and gives Beijing another reason to accelerate domestic chips, models and computing infrastructure.

Safety and compliance become businesses of their own

Mandatory model audits, red-team tests, compute disclosures, training-environment monitoring and incident reports would create demand for evaluators, cybersecurity firms and compliance software.

These businesses can grow under either outcome. A faster race requires more monitoring; stricter regulation requires more compliance.

Smaller developers and infrastructure suppliers carry more risk

The clearest pressure would fall on companies whose growth assumes a relentless expansion of AI capital spending. That group includes accelerator and custom-chip suppliers, networking vendors, new AI cloud providers and parts of the data-center construction chain.

Smaller model developers could also face a disproportionate burden. Rules tied to compute, capability or security may protect the public, but they may also concentrate the market in a few well-financed laboratories.

Hardware sold off while platforms gained

The market did not decide that AI had lost its value. It shifted value from companies selling the next unit of compute toward platforms that already own capacity and might spend less to expand it.

Fortune reported a similar interpretation: slower progress would put more pressure on infrastructure suppliers such as Nvidia and CoreWeave, while large platforms could delay construction, use existing assets and improve free cash flow.4

Investors did not price a shutdown

Amodei, Sam Altman and Elon Musk have discussed slowing the pace, but none announced an end to model training. Altman explicitly said pacing does not mean stopping.5

Investors instead appeared to price three narrower risks: longer frontier-training cycles, higher safety and compliance costs, and a shallower long-term growth curve for compute demand.

That helps explain the rise in some platform shares. Investors still expect companies to sell AI products. They are questioning whether all of the value will continue to flow to new graphics processors and data-center construction.

The market move was not a clean experiment

Oil prices, a U.S. 10-year Treasury yield near 5% and shifting interest-rate expectations also weighed on markets on Sept. 14.4 Both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq declined, so the day’s moves cannot be attributed entirely to the pacing debate.

The divergence is still notable. The semiconductor index lost 5.86%, compared with a 0.48% drop in the S&P 500, while Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta rose. The pattern supports the capex interpretation, but it does not prove causation.

4. Regulation will slow releases, not the contest

Selective restraints are more plausible than a global pause

A common moratorium remains unlikely. U.S. laboratories do not fully trust one another, and the U.S. and China have even fewer verifiable commitments. The Council on Foreign Relations describes the corporate race and the geopolitical race as two nested prisoner’s dilemmas.8

Policy is more likely to focus on procedures that governments can enforce: outside evaluations, capability thresholds, incident reporting, compute disclosures, protection of model weights and restrictions on high-risk deployment. Those rules can delay releases without reducing research budgets.

U.S. policy will combine oversight with industrial expansion

One camp in Washington will press for a single federal standard so that no laboratory is punished for voluntary caution. Another will resist hard limits that might erode the U.S. lead over China. The president has publicly argued that slowing down could endanger that lead.5

The probable compromise is tighter review of domestic models alongside more investment in power, compute and semiconductors—and tougher controls on technology flows to China. That may reduce deployment risk while leaving the national capability race intact.

China is more likely to double down on self-reliance

Beijing also worries about loss of control, cybersecurity and political stability. Chinese leaders have said AI should remain under human control, and security authorities have warned that foreign models could threaten political and ideological security.6

Those concerns do not necessarily lead to slower development. When safety proposals arrive together with restrictions on China’s access to chips and models, Beijing is more likely to treat domestic capability as a defensive necessity. The result could be two technology systems with tighter internal controls and fiercer competition between them.

Spending shifts from training scale to deployment

If frontier training faces stronger constraints, companies will put more money into making existing models cheaper, safer and more useful. Likely beneficiaries include inference optimisation, model compression, enterprise software, AI agents, security controls, power efficiency and sovereign infrastructure.

“Pacing” may therefore change the composition of AI spending without reducing the total.

5. Three paths from here

Scenario What changes Likely beneficiaries Most exposed
Selective regulation, continued competition Audits and release thresholds increase, but training investment continues Large platforms, frontier labs, safety vendors and defence AI Smaller model developers and some open-source projects
A safety incident forces a harder slowdown Governments restrict large training runs or high-risk deployments Incumbent labs, large platforms and evaluation firms Chip suppliers, new AI clouds and data-center construction
The U.S.-China race overwhelms coordination Washington resists meaningful limits and Beijing accelerates self-reliance Chips, power, sovereign compute and defence technology Global governance, cross-border cooperation and system-wide safety

Selective regulation is the most plausible baseline. A harder slowdown would probably require a major incident or clear evidence that developers had lost control of a powerful system. The first and third scenarios can also coexist: tighter rules at home and a faster race abroad.

6. What to watch

Public statements will matter less than capital budgets and enforceable rules. The clearest signals will be whether frontier laboratories delay major training runs or releases; whether cloud platforms cut capital-spending guidance; whether Washington creates a national system for model review and compute reporting; and whether China accepts any reciprocal verification.

Export controls will be equally important. New limits on chips, overseas compute rentals or model-weight transfers would show that geopolitical competition is overtaking the case for shared restraint. Any U.S.-China risk-communication mechanism would point in the opposite direction.8

7. Final assessment

The companies best placed to benefit are today’s frontier laboratories, large cloud platforms and the emerging safety-and-compliance industry. Their common advantage is scale. A regulated race still requires capital, technical expertise and the ability to satisfy complex rules.

Amodei’s stated goal is to keep AI capability from outrunning society’s ability to control it.1 The same framework could strengthen Anthropic’s safety brand, force rivals to bear similar costs, raise barriers to entry and preserve the U.S. lead over China. Those effects can all be true at once.

The market’s first reaction reflected that ambiguity. Investors marked down an assumption of permanently accelerating infrastructure demand and rewarded some platforms that could spend less while extracting more value from assets already built.4, 7

The coming contest is unlikely to be a choice between racing and stopping. Model releases may slow, market concentration may rise and the U.S.-China compute race may intensify—all while total AI spending keeps growing and shifts toward deployment, safety, efficiency and national infrastructure.
