AI 會搶走誰的飯碗? 東南亞青年就業的三種命運

AI and the Future of Youth Employment in Southeast Asia: Three Fates

以產業結構而不是單一區域標籤，理解 AI 對東南亞青年勞動力的分化影響。

Understanding the differentiated impact of AI on SEA youth employment through industrial structure, rather than one regional label.

當地球另一端的科技公司在爭論 AI 會不會取代工程師時,東南亞數以千萬計靠重複性服務工作維生的年輕人,已經在直接面對這場衝擊。但「東南亞」從來不是單一故事——一個新加坡的 PMET(專業人士、經理、行政人員及技術人員)和一個菲律賓的 call centre 客服員,面對的是完全不同的風險和選項。與其把整個區域混為一談,不如按發展階段和產業結構分成三組來看：青年勞動力密集出口型(菲律賓 vs 越南——分別靠服務業與製造業出口)、製造業轉型型(馬來西亞 vs 泰國)，以及作為政策參考的高收入對照組(新加坡)。

While tech companies on the other side of the globe debate whether AI will replace engineers, millions of young people in Southeast Asia who rely on repetitive service jobs are already directly facing this impact. However, "Southeast Asia" is never a single story—a PMET (Professionals, Managers, Executives, and Technicians) in Singapore and a call center agent in the Philippines face entirely different risks and options. Instead of lumping the entire region together, it's better to examine it in three groups based on development stages and industrial structures: youth labor-intensive export economies (Philippines vs. Vietnam—relying on service and manufacturing exports, respectively), manufacturing transformation economies (Malaysia vs. Thailand), and high-income benchmarks for policy reference (Singapore).

先搞懂：什麼是 BPO?

Understanding BPO: A Key Term

在深入數據之前,有必要先解釋一個貫穿全文的關鍵詞。BPO(Business Process Outsourcing,業務流程外判)指企業將部分營運工序外判給其他公司或國家處理,最常見的包括客戶服務熱線、技術支援、數據輸入、會計處理等重複性、規則明確的工作。菲律賓和印度是全球兩大 BPO 目的地,原因是當地英語能力強、人力成本低,數以百萬計的年輕人靠這個行業維生。BPO 之所以是觀察 AI 衝擊的最佳切入點,是因為它的工作性質——重複、規則化、可預測——恰好正是生成式 AI 最擅長取代的類型。

Before diving into the data, it's necessary to explain a key term that runs throughout this article. BPO (Business Process Outsourcing) refers to companies outsourcing parts of their operational processes to other companies or countries. The most common examples include customer service hotlines, technical support, data entry, and accounting—all repetitive, rule-based tasks. The Philippines and India are the two largest BPO destinations globally, due to strong English proficiency and low labor costs, supporting millions of young people. BPO is the best entry point for observing the impact of AI because its nature—repetitive, rule-based, predictable—is precisely what generative AI excels at replacing.

第一組：青年勞動力密集出口型——菲律賓 vs 越南

Group 1: Youth Labor-Intensive Export Economies—Philippines vs. Vietnam

兩國都擁有龐大年輕人口與中低收入水平,但產業命脈截然不同：菲律賓靠服務業出口(BPO)，越南靠製造業出口。這個差異,決定了兩國面對的其實是兩種不同機制的 AI 衝擊——菲律賓是生成式 AI 取代文字/語音客服,越南是機械人自動化取代產線工人。

Both countries have large young populations and middle-to-low income levels, but their industrial lifelines are vastly different: the Philippines relies on service exports (BPO), while Vietnam relies on manufacturing exports. This difference means they face two distinct mechanisms of AI impact—the Philippines faces generative AI replacing text/voice customer service, while Vietnam faces robotics automation replacing production line workers.

菲律賓：一個佔 GDP 7.4% 的行業在發抖

Philippines: An Industry Accounting for 7.4% of GDP is Trembling

菲律賓 BPO 行業僱用約 182 萬人,年收入約 380 億美元,佔全國 GDP 約 7.4%——這個規模與海外勞工匯款相當,意味著這個行業的任何風吹草動,都會對整體經濟造成連鎖影響。IMF 一份研究發現,菲律賓約三分之一的工人高度暴露於 AI 替代風險,而最脆弱的一群,正正是「受過大學教育、年輕、居於城市、女性」的服務業從業者——與典型 BPO 員工的畫像高度重疊。國際勞工組織(ILO)更早期的評估甚至指出,BPO 行業中近九成職位面臨高自動化風險。威脅不止來自技術本身。美國國會目前有兩項法案在審議——《Keep Call Centers in America Act》和《HIRE Act》——目標是讓企業把客服工作遷回美國本土。與 AI 自動化疊加,菲律賓 BPO 正面對「技術替代」和「政策阻力」兩面夾擊。有業界人士形容,一個 AI 客服系統已經可以同時處理 1,800 個通話。政府的應對: 菲律賓推出總值 7.4 億披索的 Project UNLAD 計劃,由通訊及資訊科技部(DICT)、技術教育及技能發展局(TESDA)與 IBPAP(菲律賓資訊科技與商業流程管理協會)合作,協助從業員升級成 AI 工具操作員、質量審核員等更高技術含量的角色。行業組織 IBPAP 仍然預期,BPO 就業人數會在 2028 年增長至 250 萬——前提是「AI 賦能型」職位的增長速度,能追得上低階職位被取代的速度。

The Philippine BPO industry employs approximately 1.82 million people, generating about $38 billion annually, accounting for roughly 7.4% of the national GDP—a scale comparable to overseas remittances. This means any fluctuation in this industry can have a ripple effect on the overall economy. An IMF study found that about one-third of Filipino workers are highly exposed to AI displacement risks, with the most vulnerable being "university-educated, young, urban, female" service sector workers—a profile highly overlapping with typical BPO employees. Earlier assessments by the International Labour Organization (ILO) even indicated that nearly 90% of BPO jobs face high automation risks. The threat extends beyond technology itself. Two bills are currently under review in the U.S. Congress—the "Keep Call Centers in America Act" and the "HIRE Act"—aiming to encourage companies to relocate customer service jobs back to the U.S. Coupled with AI automation, the Philippine BPO sector faces a double whammy of "technological displacement" and "policy resistance." Industry insiders describe an AI customer service system as capable of handling 1,800 calls simultaneously. Government Response: The Philippines launched Project UNLAD, valued at 740 million pesos, in collaboration with the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA), and IBPAP (IT & Business Process Association of the Philippines). This initiative aims to help workers upgrade to higher-skilled roles such as AI tool operators and quality assurance specialists. IBPAP still anticipates BPO employment to grow to 2.5 million by 2028—provided that the growth rate of "AI-enabled" jobs can keep pace with the displacement of lower-skilled positions.

越南：靠製造業護城河避開第一波衝擊

Vietnam: Manufacturing Moat Avoids the First Wave of Impact

越南的處境完全不同。根據越南國家統計局(GSO)官方數字,工業與建築業合計佔 GDP 約 37.65%(2025年),當中製造業出口是主要驅動力,加上半導體、電子產品出口持續擴張,英特爾、三星、LG 等企業不斷加碼當地產能。越南 2026 年 GDP per capita 預計達 5,115 美元,首次穩定超越菲律賓。有趣的是,越南年輕人對 AI 的態度相當積極——調查顯示 76% 的 35 歲以下越南人已經在使用生成式 AI 工具,AI 相關職位招聘在 2025 年增長 58%,是東盟地區增速最快的國家之一。換言之,越南年輕勞動力並非在「等待被 AI 取代」,而是主動把 AI 當成職業升級的工具。不過越南並非全無隱憂——貿易佔 GDP 比重高達近 170%,意味著這個經濟體對全球供應鏈波動極度敏感,一旦中美貿易關係或能源價格出現大幅波動,製造業出口便首當其衝。

Vietnam's situation is entirely different. According to official figures from the General Statistics Office of Vietnam (GSO), industry and construction combined accounted for approximately 37.65% of GDP (2025), with manufacturing exports being the primary driver. The continuous expansion of semiconductor and electronics exports, with companies like Intel, Samsung, and LG increasing local production capacity, further strengthens this. Vietnam's GDP per capita is projected to reach $5,115 in 2026, stably surpassing the Philippines for the first time. Interestingly, young Vietnamese are quite proactive regarding AI—surveys show that 76% of Vietnamese under 35 are already using generative AI tools, and AI-related job postings grew by 58% in 2025, making it one of the fastest-growing countries in ASEAN. In other words, young Vietnamese workers are not "waiting to be replaced by AI" but are actively using AI as a tool for career advancement. However, Vietnam is not without its concerns—trade accounts for nearly 170% of its GDP, meaning this economy is extremely sensitive to global supply chain fluctuations. Should U.S.-China trade relations or energy prices fluctuate significantly, manufacturing exports would be the first to bear the brunt.

第二組：製造業轉型型——馬來西亞 vs 泰國

Group 2: Manufacturing Transformation Economies—Malaysia vs. Thailand

兩國都試圖從中低端製造業升級至高技術含量產業,但目前處於截然不同的軌跡上。

Both countries are attempting to upgrade from low-to-mid-end manufacturing to high-tech industries, but are currently on vastly different trajectories.

馬來西亞：押注半導體，但人才缺口拖後腿

Malaysia: Betting on Semiconductors, but Talent Gap Lags

馬來西亞正透過「MADANI 經濟框架」目標在 2028 年前晉身高收入國家(人均國民總收入超過 13,845 美元)。半導體行業獲得逾 800 億令吉承諾投資,政府提供最高 100% 所得稅豁免作招商誘因。但硬傷同樣明顯：目前僅約 18% 的產業勞動力屬「技術型」人才,75% 為半技術工人,先進封裝與晶片設計等關鍵領域仍有約 5,000 個職位空缺未能填補。換言之,馬來西亞不是缺投資,而是缺「懂得操作機械人和 AI 系統的人」。

Malaysia is aiming to become a high-income nation (GNI per capita exceeding $13,845) by 2028 through the "MADANI Economy Framework." The semiconductor industry has secured over 80 billion ringgit in committed investments, with the government offering up to 100% income tax exemption as an incentive for foreign investment. However, the inherent weaknesses are equally apparent: currently, only about 18% of the industrial workforce is considered "skilled" talent, with 75% being semi-skilled workers. Key areas like advanced packaging and chip design still have approximately 5,000 unfilled job vacancies. In other words, Malaysia doesn't lack investment; it lacks "people who know how to operate robots and AI systems."

泰國：陷入「中等收入陷阱」的十字路口

Thailand: At the Crossroads of a "Middle-Income Trap"

泰國的處境較令人擔憂。世界銀行 2026 年 2 月最新報告把泰國全年增長預測下調至 1.6%,是東盟主要經濟體中最低,主因是家庭債務高達 GDP 的 86%、出口疲弱及旅遊業復甦放緩。世銀報告更直接以「勞動力危機」形容泰國處境,並敦促當局加快 AI 技能培訓——若能成功推動,相關技能提升有望為泰國 GDP 帶來最多 20% 的增長空間。泰國在 AI 採用率方面其實走在東盟前列,僅次於新加坡,工業機械人使用量亦排全球第 14。問題在於,這些技術目前主要集中在製造業,尚未大規模轉化為高附加值職位——世銀特別點出,泰國必須把 AI 應用「落地」到製造業實際生產流程,而不只是停留在採用層面。

Thailand's situation is more concerning. The World Bank's latest report in February 2026 lowered Thailand's full-year growth forecast to 1.6%, the lowest among major ASEAN economies. This is primarily due to household debt reaching 86% of GDP, weak exports, and a slowdown in tourism recovery. The World Bank report directly described Thailand's situation as a "labor crisis" and urged authorities to accelerate AI skills training—if successfully implemented, such skill enhancement could contribute up to 20% to Thailand's GDP growth. Thailand is actually at the forefront of AI adoption in ASEAN, second only to Singapore, and its industrial robot usage ranks 14th globally. The problem is that these technologies are currently concentrated in manufacturing and have not yet been widely transformed into high-value-added jobs. The World Bank specifically pointed out that Thailand must "localize" AI applications into actual manufacturing processes, rather than merely adopting them at a superficial level.

第三組：高收入對照組——新加坡的政策藍本

Third Group: High-Income Benchmark—Singapore's Policy Blueprint

新加坡不適合直接與上述四國比較——人均 GDP 差距達 10–20 倍,發展階段完全不同。但正因如此,新加坡的應對策略,反而值得作為「終局範本」參考：一個財政資源充裕的政府,可以如何系統性地處理 AI 就業衝擊?

Singapore is not suitable for direct comparison with the four countries above—its GDP per capita gap is 10–20 times higher, and its development stage is completely different. However, precisely because of this, Singapore's response strategy is worth referencing as an "ultimate blueprint": how can a fiscally well-resourced government systematically address the AI employment shock?

新加坡在做什麼

What Singapore is Doing

  • 制度整合: 2026 年第三季起,SkillsFuture Singapore 與 Workforce Singapore 將合併成新的 Skills and Workforce Development Agency(SWDA),統一技能培訓、職涯輔導與職位配對平台。
  • 真金白銀補貼: 40 歲或以上新加坡人參與認可培訓課程,政府最高資助 90% 學費。
  • 實戰工具而非紙上談兵: 2026 年下半年起,報讀指定 SkillsFuture AI 課程人士可獲六個月免費使用高階 AI 工具,直接在實際工作場景中練習應用。
  • 勞資政三方協作: 職工總會(NTUC)推出「AI-Ready SG」平台,為會員提供 AI 工具及培訓 50% 補貼。
  • Institutional Integration: From Q3 2026, SkillsFuture Singapore and Workforce Singapore will merge into a new Skills and Workforce Development Agency (SWDA), unifying skills training, career guidance, and job matching platforms.
  • Substantial Subsidies: Singaporeans aged 40 and above participating in approved training courses, government highest subsidy 90% of course fees.
  • Practical Tools, Not Just Theory: From H2 2026, individuals enrolling in designated SkillsFuture AI courses will receive six months of free access to advanced AI tools, allowing them to practice applications directly in real work scenarios.
  • Tripartite Collaboration: The National Trades Union Congress (NTUC) launched the "AI-Ready SG" platform, offering members 50% subsidies for AI tools and training.

但即使新加坡，壓力已經浮現

Even Singapore Faces Pressure

2026 年第一季新加坡整體失業率維持低企的 2.0%,但長期失業率已攀升至 0.8%——雖然數字看似細小,但這正是經濟學者眼中「結構性調整開始超越個體重新就業速度」的警號。最受衝擊的並非低技術勞工,而是金融、法律、顧問等行業中,原本仰賴「研究生級判斷力」的 PMET 白領階層。換言之,即使坐擁全球最完善的再培訓基建,新加坡依然無法完全「買斷」AI 帶來的結構性衝擊——只能盡量縮短陣痛期、分散風險。

In Q1 2026, Singapore's overall unemployment rate remained low at 2.0%, but the long-term unemployment rate climbed to 0.8%—though seemingly small, this is a warning sign for economists that "structural adjustments are beginning to outpace the speed of individual re-employment." The most affected are not low-skilled laborers, but PMET white-collar workers in finance, law, consulting, and other industries who traditionally relied on "graduate-level judgment." In other words, even with the world's most comprehensive retraining infrastructure, Singapore cannot completely "buy out" the structural impact brought by AI—it can only try to shorten the painful period and diversify risks.

未來 3–5 年：三種可能的劇本

Next 3–5 Years: Three Possible Scenarios

Based on the above data, Southeast Asian countries are broadly heading towards one of the following three scenarios between 2028–2030:

情境一・成功轉型

Scenario 1: Successful Transformation

菲律賓 BPO 成功將低階客服工作升級為「AI 輔助型」角色(如質量審核、複雜客訴處理),越南、馬來西亞持續吸納高技術製造業投資,整體區域維持正增長,但貧富差距擴大。

The Philippine BPO sector successfully upgrades low-level customer service jobs to "AI-assisted" roles (e.g., quality assurance, complex complaint handling). Vietnam and Malaysia continue to attract high-tech manufacturing investments, and the overall region maintains positive growth, but wealth disparity widens.

情境二・雙速分裂

Scenario 2: Two-Speed Divide

高技術、受過教育的年輕人成功轉型並受惠於 AI,但低技術、低學歷勞動人口被結構性淘汰,形成同一國家內部「贏家圈」與「輸家圈」的鮮明分野——這在菲律賓、泰國尤其值得留意。

High-skilled, educated young people successfully transform and benefit from AI, while low-skilled, less-educated laborers are structurally eliminated, creating a clear divide between "winners" and "losers" within the same country—this is particularly noteworthy in the Philippines and Thailand.

情境三・結構性收縮

Scenario 3: Structural Contraction

若美國外判限制立法通過,加上 AI 自動化速度快於預期,菲律賓 BPO 行業可能在 2029–2030 年間開始實質收縮,連帶影響同樣依賴海外勞工匯款的家庭經濟,形成雙重打擊。

If U.S. outsourcing restrictions are passed and AI automation accelerates faster than expected, the Philippine BPO industry could begin to substantially contract between 2029–2030, also impacting family economies reliant on overseas remittances, creating a double blow.

對香港讀者的啟示

Implications for Hong Kong Readers

這場東南亞的就業重組故事,其實離香港不遠。香港的金融後勤、法律文書處理、客戶服務、翻譯等白領工序,本質上與菲律賓 BPO 面對的風險高度相似——重複性高、規則明確、容易被生成式 AI 取代。新加坡的做法提供了一個具體參考座標：制度先行、資源到位、勞資政協作。反觀香港目前在 AI 勞動力轉型政策上的投入力度,仍有相當大的追趕空間。對於個人而言,這場變局傳遞的訊息很直接——與其等待被歸類為「哪一種情境」的一員,不如提早把自己往「AI 賦能型」而非「AI 替代型」的技能組合遷移。

This story of employment restructuring in Southeast Asia is not far from Hong Kong. Hong Kong's financial back-office, legal document processing, customer service, and translation white-collar tasks are inherently similar to the risks faced by the Philippine BPO sector—highly repetitive, rule-based, and easily replaceable by generative AI. Singapore's approach provides a concrete reference point: proactive institutional frameworks, adequate resources, and tripartite collaboration among labor, management, and government. In contrast, Hong Kong currently has significant catching-up space in its investment in AI labor transformation policies. For individuals, the message from this change is direct—instead of waiting to be categorized into "which scenario," it's better to proactively shift oneself towards an "AI-enabled" rather than "AI-displaced" skill set.
